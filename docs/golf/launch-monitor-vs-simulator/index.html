Launch monitor vs golf simulator: what's the difference?

Updated 2026-11-24. Researched from makers' published documents and Amazon UK listings. We have not tested these products.

A launch monitor is the measuring device. A simulator is a full set-up built around one, with a screen and software that show the shot and let you play virtual courses.

Launch monitor on its own

On its own, a launch monitor shows numbers, such as ball speed, launch angle and carry, on its own screen or a phone app. You can use one at a driving range, or at home into a net.

A full simulator

A home simulator adds an impact screen or net, an enclosure to catch mishits, a hitting mat, a projector or TV, and software. Some launch monitors include simulator features, sometimes on a subscription. For example, the Rapsodo MLM2PRO listing describes it as a "mobile launch monitor + golf simulator", and the Garmin R50 listing says it has a built-in 10 inch touchscreen and that virtual rounds need a subscription.

Launch monitor onlyFull simulator
What you seeShot numbers on a device or phoneThe shot on a screen, plus numbers
SpaceDepends on the unit; radar units need room behind the ballThe unit's space plus room for the screen or enclosure
Extra partsNet and mat for home useScreen, enclosure, mat, projector, software

Which to start with

You can start with a launch monitor, a net and a mat, and add a screen and projector later. If you plan to build a bay, check the room size guide first, then the simulator bay parts.

Golf launch monitors on Amazon UK

Updated 2026-11-24. Researched from makers' published documents and Amazon UK listings. We have not tested these products.

A launch monitor measures what happens when you hit the ball, such as ball speed and launch angle. Many also link to simulator software so you can play virtual courses.

Behind the ball or beside it

Where a launch monitor sits decides how much room it needs.

  • Radar units such as the Garmin Approach R10, Rapsodo MLM2PRO and Shot Scope LM1 sit on the floor behind the golfer. Garmin's R10 manual says to place it 1.8 to 2.4 m (6 to 8 ft) behind the tee. Rapsodo says 6.5 to 8.5 ft behind the ball for the MLM2PRO. Shot Scope's UK spec puts the LM1 around 1.4 to 1.5 m (55 in) behind the ball.
  • Camera units such as the Garmin Approach R50, SkyTrak ST MAX and Square Golf Omni sit beside the ball. Garmin's R50 manual says about 0.5 m (1.5 ft) to the side of the tee, facing it.

See the room size guide for each maker's figures, or open the brand comparison hub to include Uneekor, FlightScope and Bushnell/Foresight.

Subscriptions

Some features need a paid subscription. For example, the Amazon listings for the Garmin R10 and R50 say virtual rounds need an active Garmin Golf membership or subscription. By contrast, Shot Scope states its LM1 mobile app is free with no subscription. Check each maker's current terms before you buy.

Frequently asked questions

Where does a radar launch monitor sit compared with a camera unit?

Radar units such as the Garmin R10, Rapsodo MLM2PRO and Shot Scope LM1 sit 1.4 to 2.6 m behind the ball. Camera units such as the Garmin R50, SkyTrak ST MAX and Square Golf Omni sit beside the ball.

Do all launch monitors need a subscription?

No. Shot Scope LM1 uses the free Shot Scope app with no subscription. Other units, such as the Garmin R10 and R50, require an active subscription for virtual course play.
